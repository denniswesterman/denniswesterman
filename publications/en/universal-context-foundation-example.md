## The structure in brief

UCF connects what an organization knows and agrees with what an application does and what may be used as its result. This example retains the four components and five steps from the [executive paper](universal-context-foundation-executive.md). It shows their relationship through one completed application overview.

**Four components describe what the organization manages. Five steps describe how an assignment proceeds. The application overview brings together the agreements for one application.**

This does not require nine systems, a new department, or a fixed number of documents. The overview may refer to existing sources, agreements, and facilities. The technical arrangement follows from what the application needs; it is not prescribed here.

## 1. Four components under organizational control

| Component | What must be clear? | Example: preparing a policy note |
|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge and rules** | Which information and agreements apply, who manages them, and for what purposes may they be used? | The applicable policy documents, relevant sources, and confidentiality agreements. |
| **Functionality** | Which bounded task does the application support? | Preparing a draft note, not independently establishing policy. |
| **User experience** | How does someone submit an assignment and recognize errors, limitations, and approvals? | The employee can assess the draft and clearly see whether it is awaiting assessment, has been rejected, or has been released. |
| **Data structure** | Which information belongs together, who may access it, and what must be retrievable later? | The relationship between the assignment, sources used, draft version, assessment, and release. |

These components describe responsibilities, not mandatory separate storage locations. What belongs to the application stays together as much as possible. Genuinely shared knowledge and rules have a recognizable owner and source.

## 2. Five steps from assignment to responsible use

**Define the assignment → Select information → Prepare a draft → Assess the result → Release**

| Step | What happens in the example? | When may work proceed? |
|---|---|---|
| **Define the assignment** | The requester records why the policy note is needed, who will use it, and who is responsible. | Purpose, boundaries, owner, and assessment criteria are clear. |
| **Select information** | Only sources relevant to and permitted for the assignment are selected. | Applicable versions and conditions of use are known; no necessary information is missing. |
| **Prepare a draft** | AI prepares the note and makes uncertainties and missing support visible. | The result remains recognizable as a draft and is available for assessment. |
| **Assess the result** | An authorized subject-matter expert checks the supporting evidence and suitability. | The required checks have been performed, and the result has been approved for the intended use. |
| **Release** | The assessed version is made available for the agreed purpose and permitted recipients. | Authorized release is recorded and relates to this exact content and use. |

Missing necessary information or an unsatisfactory assessment does not lead to silent release. The work is supplemented, revised, or stopped. Changed content requires a new assessment; an old approval does not automatically apply to a new version.

The five steps do not mandate five AI calls or five manual checks. Checks match the risk. Human substantive assessment is mandatory in this example. Access and release are actually controlled: an instruction telling AI not to do something is insufficient on its own.

## 3. One completed application overview

### Preparing a policy note

*Fictional example based on the policy note in the executive paper. The roles described are example responsibilities, not people who have already been appointed.*

**Purpose and result**  
Prepare a draft note on energy savings, based on applicable organizational policy and permitted, relevant information. The intended result is a supported draft for assessment, not an independently made policy decision.

**Owner and assessor**  
The designated process owner is responsible for the way of working and organizes assessment. Information and policy owners oversee the applicable sources. An authorized subject-matter expert assesses the note before it may be used as approved advice.

**Knowledge and rules**  
Use the latest approved policy version applicable to this assignment and the necessary additional sources. Withdrawn documents and unnecessary confidential details are not provided. Existing sources are referenced; the application does not automatically build a second knowledge archive.

**AI's task**  
Prepare a draft that fits the assignment. The note must make clear what supports important statements and which assumptions or uncertainties still need assessment. AI receives the information necessary and permitted for this purpose, not all available organizational documentation by default.

**Boundaries**  
AI may not establish policy, expand its own permissions, or independently distribute the draft as approved advice. Missing necessary information or conflicting rules are made visible for authorized resolution. The model does not decide which obligation to discard.

**Assessment**  
The subject-matter expert assesses whether the note fits the policy, whether important conclusions are supported by the sources, and whether uncertainties are sufficiently clear. Convincing wording or successful technical execution is not approval. The assessor may reject the result or request additional supporting evidence.

**Use and release**  
Release applies only to the assessed version, the recorded purpose of use, and the permitted recipients. If content changes or conditions cease to hold, whether release is permitted must be determined again. Without the required approval, the result remains an unreleased draft.

**Accountability**  
The assignment, source versions used, result version, assessment, and release must be retrievable in relation to one another. Access and retention are arranged appropriately. This does not mean that all confidential source content is copied indefinitely. Earlier assessments remain recognizable as history.

**Exceptions and recovery**  
A missing necessary source, rejection, or unavailable supplier triggers the agreed safe way of working. If a source later proves incorrect, it must be possible to identify which notes rely on it. A revised result is assessed again; technical recovery does not automatically withdraw advice already distributed.

**Value and decision**  
The trial compares time and total cost per accepted note, substantive quality, and the operation of assessment and release. Source management, assessment, failed attempts, and correction are included. Limits for investment, risk, and assessment burden are agreed in advance. No savings, measurements, or approvals have been filled in as though they had already been achieved.

## 4. Keeping use and change simple

The application overview summarizes agreements; it is not an additional approval layer. Existing roles and systems can fulfill the responsibilities described. A reference to applicable policy is preferable to a new copy that must be maintained separately.

A change receives a targeted assessment. A clearer screen need not change policy rules. An updated source requires checks of its validity and consequences for the assignment concerned, not automatically a complete rebuild. A changed shared rule may affect multiple applications; those consequences must then be made visible.

Avoid duplicate work without necessarily reducing care. More documents, instructions, or checks are not an objective in themselves. Nor is necessary protection removed to make the arrangement appear simpler.

## 5. Demonstrating what the arrangement adds

Compare comparable assignments under the same necessary quality and safety conditions. Include the current way of working, a simple AI approach, and the proposed UCF approach. This makes the additional value of the chosen arrangement visible.

Report efficiency and control separately. More assessment time may be necessary for responsible release, but is not a proven cost saving. Also investigate whether another assessor can reconstruct execution and whether agreements remain usable with another model. Where needed, separately compare which additional instructions help, without simultaneously changing permissions or release conditions.

The outcome is a decision to stop, adapt, continue on a limited basis, or scale up. Where a simpler arrangement meets the same necessary requirements and added value is absent, simplify.

## Sources and status

This example is an executive-level elaboration of the [UCF executive paper, version 0.3](universal-context-foundation-executive.md), particularly the chapters on context management, the five steps, the four components, value, ownership, and release. The application overview is an editorial presentation format; it adds no new area of responsibility or mandatory facility. This English edition translates the Dutch example rather than introducing additional findings.

This version replaces the earlier technical example as the companion to the executive paper. It contains no prescribed folder structure, technical configuration, or description of verified software. The existing technical source publication has not been changed or re-examined.

The supplied research *Evaluating AGENTS.md* studies additional instruction files in AI programming tasks, not a complete UCF setup. It underlines the need to evaluate the effects and costs of additional instructions. The presentation and application overview chosen here are not performance improvements proven by that research.[1]

[1] Thibaud Gloaguen, Niels Mündler, Mark Müller, Veselin Raychev, and Martin Vechev, *Evaluating AGENTS.md: Are Repository-Level Context Files Helpful for Coding Agents?*, preprint, [arXiv:2602.11988v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988v2), 23 June 2026. Source used: the supplied PDF *Evaluating AGENTS.pdf*, particularly pp. 5-9 and 16-17. The study does not show a statistically significant general improvement in task success from adding these files compared with no additional file; costs do increase. The same limits on interpretation are retained in the executive paper.
